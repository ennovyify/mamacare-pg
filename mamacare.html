🥗 Nutrition Guidance
🌿 Key Nutrients You Need
  • Folic Acid (400–800 mcg/day) — prevents neural tube defects. Found in leafy greens, beans, fortified cereals.
  • Iron (27 mg/day) — supports baby's blood and brain. Lean red meat, spinach, lentils — take with vitamin C.
  • Calcium (1,000 mg/day) — builds baby's bones. Dairy, fortified OJ, broccoli, almonds.
  • DHA / Omega-3 — brain development. Salmon, sardines, walnuts, DHA-fortified eggs.
  • Vitamin D (600 IU/day) — bone health. Sunlight + fortified milk + supplements.
  • Protein (75–100g/day) — builds baby's tissues. Eggs, chicken, beans, Greek yogurt.
💡 Take a prenatal vitamin every day. Ask your WIC office for free prenatal vitamins — (301) 883-7870.
🍽️ What to Eat Each Day
  • Colorful fruits & vegetables — aim for 5+ servings
  • Whole grains — oatmeal, brown rice, whole wheat bread
  • Lean protein — eggs, beans, chicken, fish (avoid shark/swordfish)
  • Dairy or fortified alternatives — milk, yogurt, cheese
  • Healthy fats — avocado, nuts, olive oil
  • Water — 8–10 cups daily to stay hydrated and reduce swelling
🚫 Foods to Avoid
  • Raw or undercooked meat, fish, eggs
  • High-mercury fish: shark, swordfish, king mackerel, tilefish
  • Unpasteurized dairy and juices
  • Deli meats and hot dogs (unless heated until steaming)
  • Alcohol — no safe amount during pregnancy
  • Limit caffeine to under 200 mg/day (about 1 small coffee)
💡 WIC provides free milk, eggs, fruits, vegetables, whole grains & juice. Apply if you haven't already!
🤢 Managing Morning Sickness
  • Eat small meals every 2–3 hours — empty stomach makes nausea worse
  • Keep crackers by your bed — eat a few before getting up
  • Ginger tea or ginger candies can help
  • Avoid greasy, spicy, or strong-smelling foods
  • Stay hydrated — sip water, broth, or electrolyte drinks
  • Vitamin B6 (10–25 mg) may help — ask your provider
💪 Recovering After Birth
  • Iron-rich foods — rebuild blood lost during delivery: spinach, beans, red meat, fortified cereal
  • Protein — repairs tissues: eggs, chicken, fish, legumes, Greek yogurt
  • Fiber — prevents postpartum constipation: fruits, veggies, whole grains, water
  • Calcium — especially important if breastfeeding: dairy, fortified foods, leafy greens
  • Vitamin C — supports wound healing: citrus, strawberries, bell peppers
💡 Continue your prenatal vitamin for at least 6 months postpartum, especially if breastfeeding.
🍼 Nutrition While Breastfeeding
  • You need about 400–500 extra calories per day while breastfeeding
  • Drink water every time you nurse — hydration is key
  • Keep eating DHA-rich foods for baby's brain development through breast milk
  • Limit caffeine to 1–2 cups/day
  • Some babies are sensitive to garlic or dairy in your diet — watch for fussiness
💡 WIC supports breastfeeding moms with extra food benefits & free lactation support — (301) 883-7870.
😔 Food & Mood: Postpartum Mental Health
  • Eat regularly — skipping meals drops blood sugar and worsens mood
  • Omega-3 rich foods support brain health: fish, walnuts, flaxseed
  • Limit sugar and ultra-processed foods that cause energy crashes
  • Stay hydrated — dehydration causes fatigue and irritability
💡 Feeling overwhelmed for more than 2 weeks? Reach out to Healthy Beginnings at (301) 883-7830.
🤱 Breastfeeding Basics
  • Feed on demand — newborns typically eat every 1.5–3 hours (8–12 times/day)
  • Each feeding: 10–20 minutes per breast
  • Signs baby is getting enough: 6+ wet diapers/day, steady weight gain
  • Cluster feeding is normal — especially during growth spurts
💡 WIC offers free breast pumps and lactation counselors. Call (301) 883-7870.
🍼 Formula Feeding
  • Use iron-fortified infant formula for the first 12 months
  • Do NOT water down formula — it lacks nutrients when diluted
  • Feed on demand — newborns take 1.5–3 oz every 2–3 hours
  • Never prop a bottle — always hold baby during feeding
  • WIC provides free formula — call (301) 883-7870
🥣 Starting Solid Foods (4–6 Months)
  • Wait for readiness: sits with support, head control, interest in food
  • Start with single-ingredient purees: sweet potato, squash, peas, fruits
  • Introduce one new food at a time, wait 3–5 days before another
  • No honey before 12 months (risk of botulism)
  • No cow's milk as main drink before 12 months
💡 WIC covers baby food & cereal for infants 6–12 months. Use all your WIC benefits!

📝
How to Apply for CCS Through DSS
Step-by-Step Guide
Apply Guide
Step 1 — Check eligibility: you must be working, in school, or in job training. Child must be under 13 (or under 19 if special needs).

Step 2 — Gather documents: photo ID, proof of income (pay stubs or award letter), proof of residency (utility bill or lease), child's birth certificate, and proof of school/work enrollment.

Step 3 — Apply at PG County DSS (805 Taylor St, Hyattsville), call (301) 699-8900, or apply online at marylandbenefits.gov.

Step 4 — Choose a licensed provider that accepts CCS. Search at marylandchildcare.org.

Step 5 — Renew annually. DSS will send a renewal notice — respond promptly to avoid gaps in coverage.
📞 (301) 699-8900🌐 marylandbenefits.gov
